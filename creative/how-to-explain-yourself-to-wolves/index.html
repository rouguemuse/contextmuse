Project Overviews

How to Explain Yourself to Wolves

COMPLETED MANUSCRIPT Representation inquiries welcome
An allegorical memoir about the exhausting work of translating yourself for people determined to misunderstand you—and what happens when explanation is no longer a path to safety.
I. Author Statement

This manuscript grew from a need to map out boundaries where direct communication had broken down. I wanted to examine the point where translation stops being a tool for mutual understanding and instead becomes a defensive mechanism, an exhausting operational loop that yields no safety.

By moving away from literal timelines and into an allegorical framework, I was able to examine the structural mechanics of communication failures, neurodivergent masking, and high-pressure family dynamics. The book uses a consistent system of symbols to examine boundaries, self-explanation, and the ultimate necessity of stepping off the mapped roads entirely.

II. The Landscape

A world built from roads, weather, maps, rooms, lanterns, and wolves.

The narrative operates within a structured, symbolic topology where emotional and psychological dynamics are represented as physical constraints:

  • The Wolves Represent forces of persistent misunderstanding, logical coercion, and the demanding expectation of absolute self-explanation as a condition of safety.
  • The Cartographers Represent the intellectual attempt to map out boundaries, establish logic schemas, and find safety through structured systems and definitions.
  • The Lanterns Represent the tools of self-explanation—the constant output of logical summaries and translated files created to prove compliance or safety.
  • The Roads & Weather Represent the social structures, institutional expectations, and environmental conditions that dictate how and where an individual is allowed to travel.
IV. Themes

Core Explorations

The memoir examines a network of connected human and structural themes:

  • ■ Neurodivergence & Masking
  • ■ Coercive Systems & Control
  • ■ Motherhood & Protective Instincts
  • ■ Memory & Narrative Structure
  • ■ Structural Translation Limits
  • ■ Silence & Boundary Limits

VI. Design Relevance

About the Writing Process

Structuring a book-length manuscript requires the same editorial discipline as designing complex software directories. It is an exercise in long-form narrative architecture, managing conceptual continuity, establishing logical parameters, and designing cohesive systems of language.
