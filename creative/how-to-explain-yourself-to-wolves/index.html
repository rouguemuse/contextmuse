Project Overviews

How to Explain Yourself to Wolves

PROJECT ADAPTATION Representation inquiries welcome
An allegorical memoir about the exhausting work of translating yourself for people determined to misunderstand you—and what happens when explanation is no longer a path to safety.
I. Project Overview

This manuscript began with a question: what happens when explanation stops creating understanding and becomes a condition of belonging or safety? By moving beyond a literal chronology and into allegory, the book creates a symbolic landscape for examining identity, motherhood, memory, repeated misunderstanding, and the decision to stop translating oneself for people committed to a different story.

II. The Symbolic Landscape

A world built from roads, weather, maps, rooms, lanterns, and wolves.

The narrative operates within a structured, symbolic topology where emotional and psychological dynamics are represented as physical constraints:

  • The Wolves Represent forces of persistent misunderstanding and the expectation of continuous self-explanation as a condition of safety.
  • The Cartographers Represent the attempt to understand boundaries and find clarity through writing and structure.
  • The Lanterns Represent the tools of self-explanation—the explanations created to bridge understanding.
  • The Roads & Weather Represent the social structures and life environments that shape how and where a person is allowed to move.
III. Core Themes

Core Explorations

The memoir examines a network of connected human and structural themes:

  • ■ Neurodivergence & Masking
  • ■ Social Systems & Control
  • ■ Motherhood & Protective Instincts
  • ■ Memory & Narrative Structure
  • ■ The Limits of Translation
  • ■ Silence & Boundary Limits
