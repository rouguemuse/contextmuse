Product & Experience Design

Interfaces are only useful when the structure behind them makes sense.

My product and experience work begins before visual styling. I examine the users, decisions, information, constraints, and failure points involved, then design an interface that makes the underlying system easier to understand and use.

Case Study 01

Signal

WORKING PROTOTYPE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE DECISION SUPPORT

A. Overview

Context: Restaurant and retail hospitality operations generating high-volume, multi-layered transaction records daily.

Problem: Shift overrides, labor margin inefficiencies, and audit anomalies are buried deep within raw, unorganized Toast POS log exports, meaning they are missed by busy general managers.

Users: General Managers and Operational Directors seeking to optimize labor costs and identify shift leakage quickly.

B. System Design

Constraints: Ingest files vary in completeness and layout structure depending on active shifts and override logging profiles.

Information Architecture: Raw input fields are parsed, normalized into a consistent database schema, and mapped against a severity threshold scorecard.

Interaction Design: The dashboard organizes findings into a clean "to-fix" checklist with expandable audit details and source citations.

C. Decisions and Build

Design Decisions: Prioritized text readability and immediate contrast on shift indicators over dense, complex charts to reduce GM cognitive load during busy shifts.

Prototype: Built a local parsing prototype that extracts override and labor anomalies into interactive shift queues.

D. Boundaries and Application

Boundaries: Signal operates purely as a decision-support tool. It does not automate employee disciplinary actions, scheduling changes, or POS corrections; all flagged items require validation by a human general manager.

Transferability: Highly useful for cash-drawer audits in retail, shift logs in warehousing, or dispatch records in logistics.

Limitations: Currently designed for manual exports from POS tools.

Case Study 02

GenSort

WORKING PROTOTYPE LOCAL-FIRST WORKSPACE METADATA LINEAGE

A. Overview

Context: Large local digital workspaces holding thousands of generated images, photos, and project assets.

Problem: Traditional file systems lack visual-relationship indexing or prompt lineage tracking, forcing designers to spend hours manually searching and categorizing files.

Users: Graphic designers, content creators, and independent artists seeking library organization.

B. System Design

Constraints: Must operate entirely offline without third-party cloud uploads to ensure asset copyright preservation and complete data control.

Information Architecture: Builds a local catalog tracking file properties, color summaries, similarity tags, and prompt/seed inheritance.

Interaction Design: Minimizes visual clutter with a two-pane layout: a high-density thumbnail grid and an expandable metadata drawer.

C. Decisions and Build

Design Decisions: Used a grid system that supports smart-folder grouping, permitting users to instantly isolate duplicates and track prompt lineages.

Prototype: Developed a desktop-focused offline directory scanner and categorizer.

D. Boundaries and Application

Boundaries: Files are never modified, moved, or deleted automatically. GenSort recommends groupings but requires explicit human drag-and-drop approval.

Transferability: The folder-scanning and metadata-association logic can be adapted to manage and index high-volume text databases or source-code archives.

Case Study 03

Self Cartography

RESEARCH SYSTEM INFORMATION MODELING CONCEPT VISUALIZATION

A. Overview

Context: Organizing dense, complex personal narratives, conceptual models, and thematic structures during creative mapping phases.

Problem: Ideas and conceptual boundaries are highly abstract, fragmented, and hard to track across standard textual notes, leading to conceptual breaks.

Users: Writers, researchers, and conceptual developers mapping out narrative environments.

B. System Design

Constraints: Must preserve the complex, non-linear relationships of themes without converting them into a flat document checklist.

Information Architecture: Implements a structured taxonomy of symbols (e.g., boundaries, gates, lanterns) with a unified glossary database schema.

Interaction Design: Visualizes topics as a network node web or structured terminology index.

C. Decisions and Build

Design Decisions: Chose a cartographic visual theme with clean mono typography and red-thread highlight links to map relationships between entries.

Prototype: Developed structural maps, thematic tables, and a conceptual glossary index.

D. Boundaries and Application

Boundaries: It structures and visualizes concepts; it does not automate writing, narrative choices, or intellectual leaps.

Transferability: Directly applicable to game level planning, complex documentation wikis, or project management taxonomy frameworks.

Case Study 04

Resource Guide Compilation

DEMO DIRECTORY RESEARCH SYNTHESIS PUBLIC-INTEREST DESIGN

A. Overview

Context: Public assistance directories and community support databases where information is highly fragmented and constantly changing.

Problem: Public resource lists are typically unstructured lists of PDFs or links with varying details, making it slow and confusing for people seeking help to find eligibility rules.

Users: Case managers, community operators, and individuals seeking assistance directories.

B. System Design

Constraints: Must normalize highly inconsistent details (varying costs, eligibility documents, intakes) into a unified schema.

Information Architecture: Developed a 14-field schema (Category, Org, Geography, Eligibility, Required Docs, Verification Date, etc.).

Interaction Design: Designed a searchable directory dashboard with instant text filters, category selectors, and a responsive table display.

C. Decisions and Build

Design Decisions: Featured in both Creative Work and Product & Experience Design because the project combines public-interest research, content systems, taxonomy design, and interface development.

Prototype: Developed an interactive JavaScript dashboard using clean, synthetic demonstration records.

D. Boundaries and Application

Boundaries: Resource details can change. The dashboard organizes and summarizes data but does not replace verification with listed providers or professionals.

Transferability: Directly applicable to community asset mapping, corporate directory intranets, or inventory catalogs.
