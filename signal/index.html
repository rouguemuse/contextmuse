I // MARKET POSITIONING

Restaurants already have dashboards. Signal is built for the step after reporting.

Independent restaurants, multi-unit groups, and hospitality managers are rarely short on reporting. They drown in POS shift summaries, labor percentage reports, discount spreadsheets, and schedule audits that live in isolated tabs and never speak to each other.

Signal asks what happens when signals from those dashboards intersect, which findings are financially meaningful, which are trustworthy, and which are actually worth acting on.

A beautifully analyzed list of 25 problems is still 25 problems. Signal is designed to reduce the list.

CRITICAL POSITIONING

The difference between numbers, trends, and operational decisions.

Most restaurant management software ends at retrospective reporting. Signal begins where traditional software stops: translating operational drift into prioritized interventions.

A NORMAL REPORT TELLS YOU:
“Labor is 22%.”

A single, isolated historical metric. It records what happened in gross terms, but gives no operational context, daypart timing, or concurrent kitchen bottlenecks.

A BETTER DASHBOARD TELLS YOU:
“Labor increased 2.4 points.”

A directional trend line. It establishes that drift occurred over time, but leaves the operator guessing where it leaked, why it occurred, and what else broke simultaneously.

SIGNAL AIMS TO ANSWER:
“The increase is concentrated in Friday/Saturday 6–8 PM dinner rushes, overlaps a 68% void spike and 18% throughput drop, represents ~$1,500/mo exposure, and testing station deployment has lower friction than adding another shift employee.”

Connecting patterns, checking facts against assumptions, and weighing how hard fixes are before modifying your floor operations.

“Is the additional discount expense from an uncapped promotion producing enough incremental value to justify itself?”

Signal Snapshot (Level 1): “You have one question. We dig into it.” A focused review that balances money at stake against how hard things are to fix, giving you a clear list of what to tackle first.

What reports and data does each level require?

Level 0 requires only 1–2 standard reports you already have (30–60d sales summary, hourly sales, or voids). Level 1 adds labor and menu mix data. Level 2 integrates check-level detail, timecards, modifier logs, and kitchen pacing timestamps. You can upload exports yourself or provide temporary reporting-only access.

What kinds of questions can each level answer?

Level 0 answers “Is there something in my data worth looking at?” Level 1 answers “Where should I look first?” Level 2 answers “Why is it happening, what is it costing, and what is the least disruptive useful thing to test first?” Ongoing Review answers “Did the change actually work?”

What does the Diagnostic discover that the Snapshot cannot?

The Snapshot establishes which problems matter most. The Diagnostic investigates competing explanations (e.g. testing whether a rush slowdown is caused by understaffing, kitchen line bottlenecks, or menu friction), explicitly distinguishes correlation from causation, and designs targeted floor experiments before you alter staffing.

Why would I pay for the deeper Diagnostic?

Because in a working restaurant, acting on the wrong explanation can be far more expensive than the problem itself. Adding a cook to solve a throughput problem can overcrowd the line and inflate payroll without fixing ticket pacing. The Diagnostic models friction and evidence strength before you spend money altering procedures.
