I // MARKET POSITIONING

Restaurants already have dashboards. Signal is built for the step after reporting.

Independent restaurants, multi-unit groups, and hospitality managers are rarely short on reporting. They drown in POS shift summaries, labor percentage reports, discount spreadsheets, and schedule audits that live in isolated tabs and never speak to each other.

Signal asks what happens when signals from those dashboards intersect, which findings are financially meaningful, which are trustworthy, and which are actually worth acting on.

A beautifully analyzed list of 25 problems is still 25 problems. Signal is designed to reduce the list.

WHAT REPORTING DASHBOARDS DO

Display historical metrics: sales totals, labor cost percentages, total comps, void counts, and item sales counts in separate silos. They tell you what recorded, but leave you to connect the dots across spreadsheets.

WHAT SIGNAL DOES

Cross-analyzes multiple operational files simultaneously, weighs exposure against implementation friction, and compresses dozens of potential issues into a prioritized decision queue with clear verification steps.

II // CORE DIFFERENTIATION

Looking at intersections, not isolated spreadsheets.

Signal does not evaluate labor in a vacuum or count voids on an island. Operational breakdown in hospitality almost always happens at the seams between systems. Signal identifies where patterns across multiple operations collide:

EPISTEMIC SEPARATION

Separating evidence from hypothesis.

Every explanation in Signal separates undisputed facts from operational inferences. Signal strictly avoids unsupported causal assertions. Instead, the system reports what the submitted data directly establishes, what the pattern is consistent with, and what requires operator verification on the floor.

01 // OBSERVED

Direct Evidence

What the submitted records directly establish as empirical facts: timestamps, check amounts, void totals, roster hours, and modifier logs that exist without interpretation.

02 // INFERRED

Operational Pattern

What the combined evidence reasonably suggests. Statistical overlaps, temporal clusters, and recurring correlations labeled with explicit confidence ratings (Low, Moderate, High).

03 // VERIFY

Operator Floor Check

What needs to be checked by an operator before accepting the explanation. Targeted, 15-minute floor audits to confirm whether operational reality matches the data pattern.

04 // ACTION CANDIDATE

Targeted Remedy

What may be worth changing if real-world validation supports it. Specific, low-friction adjustments to shift prep, line pacing, or POS button configuration.

III // DECISION PRIORITIZATION

Weighing financial exposure against operational disruption.

A theoretically large opportunity should not automatically rank first if the evidence is weak or the operational disruption required to act is too high. In a working restaurant, disrupting a functioning kitchen line or service rhythm for an unconfirmed hunch can cause more damage than the leak itself.

Signal exists to answer seven practical operational questions before any change is made:

Q1 What matters most?
Q2 What is the evidence?
Q3 How sure are we?
Q4 What needs human verification?
Q5 What is it likely worth?
Q6 How difficult is it to fix?
Q7 What should be checked first?
1. Financial Exposure

Estimated dollar leak per month or service based on recorded check values and hours.

2. Evidence Strength

Statistical confidence rated Low, Moderate, or High based on sample size and signal overlap.

3. Human Validation

The exact floor observations required to confirm the pattern before changing policy.

4. Implementation Friction

Rated Low, Moderate, or High reflecting staff retraining, menu changes, or kitchen pushback.

5. Decision Priority

Final queue placement: balancing exposure, confidence, and friction to surface the immediate next step.

PRIORITY 01 Peak-period margin leakage

Category: Throughput & Kitchen Timing
Estimated Exposure ~$1,200 – $1,800 / mo
Evidence Confidence Moderate (72%)
Implementation Friction Moderate
Decision Priority Rank 1 • Investigate First
Observed

A disproportionate share of void value (68%) and kitchen overtime (41%) clusters strictly within the same operating window: Friday and Saturday peak dinner services between 7:45 PM and 9:15 PM.

Inferred

The overlap is consistent with a peak-period line throughput bottleneck where ticket backup leads to table cancellations and comps. However, the current POS records do not establish causation.

Verify

Compare void timestamps against ticket durations, floor staffing rosters, and void reason detail for the last 3 peak Saturday services to confirm whether kitchen delays preceded the check voids.

Action Candidate

If floor verification confirms the pacing bottleneck, adjust expo prep sequencing 30 minutes prior to peak rush and rebalance fry/grill ticket pacing rather than hiring additional staff.

Why This Ranks First: High recurring monthly exposure with clear temporal concentration. While implementation friction is moderate (requires shift lead alignment), the verification step takes under 15 minutes of floor observation before any procedure is modified.

VII // SYSTEM BOUNDARIES

What do I give you?

You provide standard operational exports: transaction logs, point-of-sale (POS) spreadsheets, timecard reports, labor rosters, void reports, or scheduling audits covering 30 to 90 days. We accept raw CSV, Excel, or JSON dumps directly from your system without requiring API access.

What does Signal analyze?

Signal cross-analyzes operations across key risk areas: transaction comps/void patterns, throughput bottlenecks, scheduling vs. revenue timing, menu modifier anomalies, and high-frequency discount leaks across shift windows.

What do I receive?

You receive a clear, plain-language diagnostic audit. It lists prioritized operating issues, estimated financial impact and measurable improvement targets, and a floor-validation script to verify findings before changing active procedures.

How much does it cost?
