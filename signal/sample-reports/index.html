03 // Detailed Findings & Recommended Actions

F-01: Friday and Saturday dinner is backing up the kitchen

Investigate First
Finding ID: F-01 Certainty: Modeled (Needs in-store check) How Hard to Fix: Low to Medium Area: Dinner Rush & Kitchen Overtime
What We Found
During Friday and Saturday dinner between 6:00 PM and 8:30 PM, table turn speed drops by 18%. In that exact same two-and-a-half-hour window, the kitchen logged 318.5 overtime hours ($6,846 in extra pay) and managers comped or voided 42 entire checks ($1,180 total) because guests waited too long or meals came out cold.
Why It Matters
Labor costs spike at the exact moment the kitchen slows down. Staff is burning overtime trying to catch up, but tickets are still taking over 28 minutes to hit tables. The restaurant is paying premium wages while guests are having bad experiences and walking away with free meals.
What It May Be Costing
In Your Records
$8,026 / 60d
$6,846 overtime + $1,180 rush voids
Estimated Monthly Loss
$2,800 – $4,500
Includes 1 to 2 lost table turns per station
Yearly Risk
$33,600 – $54,000
If rush slowdown continues unfixed
How Sure Are We?
MODELED We know the overtime hours and void numbers are 100% accurate because they come straight from your payroll and POS. However, before assuming kitchen delays are the sole cause of the voids, an operator needs to watch the kitchen pass during a busy shift.
What to Check
On the next two busy Fridays, have a manager stand at the kitchen pass between 6:30 PM and 7:30 PM for 20 tickets. Watch whether the saute or grill line is running out of prepped items and waiting on backups mid-rush.
What to Do Next
• First move: Move 2.5 prep hours per day from the chaotic evening rush to a dedicated morning prep shift (8:00 AM – 10:30 AM) paid at regular pay instead of overtime.
• Second move: Temporarily pause or throttle delivery tablets if more than 16 active dine-in orders are on the line between 6:30 PM and 7:30 PM.
How we got here (Data sources, math & calculations)
Source Reports: labor_punch_records.csv, pos_void_log.csv, hourly sales summary.
Calculations: 318.5 OT hours × $21.50 OT pay differential = $6,846.00. 42 peak voids / 68 total voids = 61.8% of counts ($1,180.00 / $1,840.00 total value = 64.1%). Peak ticket times averaged 28.6 min vs 14.2 min weekday baseline.
Ledger Reference: CLM-01-01 (Observed), CLM-01-02 (Observed), CLM-01-03 (Inferred).

F-02: The 10% promotion is giving away more than the $5 offer

Change This Week
Finding ID: F-02 Certainty: Certain (Verified from POS) How Hard to Fix: Very Easy (5 minutes) Area: Promotion Settings & Discounts
What We Found
The restaurant ran two offers: "$5 off when you spend $50+" and "10% off everything." Both offers give the exact same $5 discount on a $50 bill. But on larger checks, the 10% offer gave away substantially more money ($10 on a $100 check, $15 on a $150 check). Across 940 redemptions, the 10% offer gave away $7,900 in discounts compared to $4,400 across 880 redemptions for the $5 offer.
Why It Matters
Customers using the 10% offer only spent $9.50 more on average than customers using the $5 offer ($84 vs $74.50), but took an extra $3.40 in discount. Once food cost on the extra items is factored in, the 10% offer only generated $2.30 in net contribution per table while burning $3,500 in extra discount expense. You gave away margin to guests who would have spent that money anyway.
What It May Be Costing
In Your Records
$3,500 / 60d
Excess discount vs capped structure
Monthly Giveback
$1,750 / mo
Unnecessary discount dollars
Annual Savings
$21,000 / yr
Recaptured with 1 POS setting change
How Sure Are We?
VERIFIED We calculated this directly from the 1,820 promotion checks in your POS records. Every discount dollar and pre-discount check size is recorded.
What to Check
Check whether guests who redeem the 10% offer are regular diners or ordering higher-end specials that would have sold without a discount.
What to Do Next
Change the 10% discount button in your POS to have a $5 or $7.50 maximum cap (or change it to "$5 off purchases of $50+"). Run it for 30 days and watch if order volume or check sizes drop. (Usually, they do not).
How we got here (Data sources, math & calculations)
Source Reports: pos_discount_detail.csv, check distribution summary.
Calculations: Offer B redemptions (940 checks × $8.40 avg discount = $7,900) vs Offer A (880 checks × $5.00 avg discount = $4,400). Delta = $3,500.00 excess discount. Pre-discount check lift of +$9.50 gross check minus $3.40 extra discount and $3.80 food cost leaves only +$2.30 net contribution.
Ledger Reference: CLM-02-01 (Observed), CLM-02-02 (Derived).

F-03: Some paid extras are being given away for free or below cost

Quick Win
Finding ID: F-03 Certainty: Certain (Verified from POS & Recipes) How Hard to Fix: Very Easy (10 minutes) Area: Menu Prices & Add-Ons
What We Found
Four popular add-ons appeared on 3,240 guest orders: extra bacon, truffle fries upgrade, extra cheese, and gluten-free buns. The price charged to guests in the POS was set months ago and has not kept up with rising food prices:
  • Extra Bacon: Charged at +$1.00, but the portion costs you $1.75 to prepare.
  • Truffle Fries Upgrade: Charged at +$1.50, but costs you $2.60.
  • Extra Cheese: Charged at +$0.50, but costs you $0.95.
  • Gluten-Free Bun: Charged at +$1.00, but costs you $1.80.
Why It Matters
Your base entrees make a healthy 68% gross margin. But when guests add one of these four extras, the margin drops to 54–58%. Because these four add-ons are ordered on over a third of your specialty entrees, you are quietly losing money on your most popular dishes.
What It May Be Costing
In Your Records
$2,443 / 60d
Direct profit loss on 3,240 extras
Monthly Profit Loss
$1,221 / mo
Pure gross margin leak
Annual Profit Gain
$14,650 / yr
Recovered by updating 4 POS buttons
How Sure Are We?
VERIFIED We compared the 3,240 itemized modifier lines from your POS against distributor invoice prices and recipe portion cards.
What to Check
Verify current prices for bacon, dairy, and gluten-free bread on your latest distributor invoice to confirm our cost numbers match your current supplier rates.
What to Do Next
Do not reprint or redesign the whole menu. Simply update the 4 modifier prices in your POS backend: Bacon to $2.00, Truffle Fries to $2.75, Extra Cheese to $1.00, and Gluten-Free Bun to $2.00.
How we got here (Data sources, math & calculations)
Source Reports: modifier_sales_log.csv, distributor invoices, recipe cost cards.
Calculations: Bacon (-$0.75 × 1,420 = -$1,065) + Truffle Fries (-$1.10 × 860 = -$946) + Extra Cheese (-$0.45 × 960 = -$432) = $2,443.00 net margin loss.
Ledger Reference: CLM-03-01 (Observed), CLM-03-02 (Derived).

F-04: Shift meals are being entered as manager comps

Process Fix
Finding ID: F-04 Certainty: Probable (Needs quick check) How Hard to Fix: Easy (15 minutes) Area: Staff Meal Policy & POS Keys
What We Found
A manager discount code named MGR-COMP-99 was used 142 times for a total of $2,840. But 82 of those orders ($1,640) happened between 3:30 PM and 4:30 PM or after midnight closing, and almost all of them were for a single sandwich or beverage on a solo ticket.
Why It Matters
This does not look like theft. It looks like shift supervisors and line cooks are using the general manager emergency comp code to record their employee break meals because there is no "Staff Meal" button set up in the POS. When employee meals are mixed into manager comps, owners cannot see whether customer service comps are actually under control.
What It May Be Costing
In Your Records
$1,640 / 60d
82 off-peak meal checks
Monthly Giveback
$820 / mo
Unmonitored meal discounts
Resolution Path
Procedure Fix
Zero staff friction or write-ups
How Sure Are We?
NEEDS VALIDATION The timing and single-item tickets strongly suggest employee shift meals, but an operator should ask a shift supervisor before taking any disciplinary action.
What to Check
Ask your shift leads what button they press when a cook or dishwasher orders a meal during break. Compare clock-out times against the timestamps on the largest MGR-COMP-99 checks.
What to Do Next
• Create a dedicated "Employee Shift Meal" button in the POS with preset rules (e.g. 50% discount or $10 daily allowance).
• Reserve the MGR-COMP-99 code strictly for guest service recovery (like long waits or cold food).
• Train shift leads at the next team huddle. No disciplinary action needed.
How we got here (Data sources, math & calculations)
Source Reports: pos_discount_journal.csv, employee shift punches.
Calculations: MGR-COMP-99: 142 total checks ($2,840.00). 82 checks ($1,640.00) timed exactly with shift turnovers on single-cover checks ($20.00 average).
Ledger Reference: CLM-04-01 (Observed), CLM-04-02 (Inferred).
