03 // Canonical Synthesized Findings

F-01 — Peak-Period Throughput / Margin Leakage

Investigate First
Finding ID: F-01 Confidence: Moderate Implementation Friction: Low to Moderate Classification: Multi-Signal Intersection
Observed
Kitchen overtime reached 318.5 hours ($6,846 overtime premium) across the 60-day period. 42 of 68 manager voids ($1,180 of $1,840 total void value, averaging $28.10) concentrated during Friday and Saturday dinner services between 6:00 PM and 8:30 PM, where check velocity dropped by 18% relative to midday baseline.
Intersection
Kitchen overtime payroll spikes co-occur precisely with full-ticket service recovery voids and ticket latency spikes during peak weekend dinner volume (6:00–8:30 PM), revealing that labor expenditure increases at the exact window where kitchen throughput deteriorates.
Inferred
Kitchen line saturation during the weekend dinner peak creates compounding ticket delays exceeding 28 minutes, forcing front-of-house managers to execute full-ticket comp voids ($28.10 avg) to salvage guest relations while back-of-house staff burns premium overtime rates attempting to clear the backlog.
Confidence
Moderate. High confidence on POS void timestamps and payroll overtime hours; moderate confidence on direct causality between kitchen delay and voids until pass fire-to-window logs are audited on the floor.

F-02 — Promotion Structure Leakage

Execute Test
Finding ID: F-02 Confidence: High Implementation Friction: Very Low Focus: Capped vs Uncapped Promotion Economics
Observed
Offer A ($5 off $50+) was redeemed 880 times, totaling $4,400 in discounts ($5.00 fixed discount, pre-discount check average $74.50). Offer B (10% off uncapped) was redeemed 940 times, totaling $7,900 in discounts ($8.40 average discount, pre-discount check average $84.00). Above $50, Offer B discounts scaled linearly ($100 check = $10 discount; $150 check = $15 discount).
Intersection
Check-size distribution crossed with promotion redemption records establishes that Offer B cost $3,500 more than Offer A (+79.5% discount expense) while producing only $9.50 in higher gross check spend ($84.00 vs $74.50), yielding zero statistically meaningful expansion in high-margin dessert or beverage attachment.
Inferred
The uncapped 10% promotion is subsidizing high-spend diners who would have spent $75+ regardless. The additional $3,500 discount expense functions as margin transfer rather than demand generation.
Confidence
High. Complete POS transactional capture across 1,820 promotion checks with unambiguous dollar and check distribution data.

F-03 — Modifier Pricing Erosion

Plan Menu Update
Finding ID: F-03 Confidence: High Implementation Friction: Very Low Principle: Right-Sized Intervention
Observed
Four high-frequency menu modifiers (Add Bacon, Sub Truffle Fries, Extra Cheese, Gluten-Free Bun) appeared on 3,240 checks across 60 days. POS pricing: Bacon +$1.00 (ingredient + prep delta $1.75), Truffle Fries +$1.50 (cost delta $2.60), Extra Cheese +$0.50 (cost delta $0.95), GF Bun +$1.00 (cost delta $1.80). Base entrees maintain 68% gross margin, but modified entrees drop to 54–58% margin.
Intersection
Cross-referencing food cost recipe sheets with item modifier sales reveals that the top selling entrees maintain healthy margins until specific high-volume customizations are selected, eroding up to 14% margin points per dish.
Inferred
Modifier prices were set historically without indexing against recent supplier ingredient inflation. Because these four modifications are selected on 34.4% of all specialty entree orders, modifier underpricing creates systemic margin leakage without driving incremental guest satisfaction.
Confidence
High. Recipe cost cards verified against vendor invoices and cross-matched with 3,240 POS modifier item lines.

F-04 — Discount Procedure Leakage

Investigate First
Finding ID: F-04 Confidence: Moderate Implementation Friction: Low Principle: Separate People Touching from People Causing
Observed
Manager discount code 'MGR-COMP-99' was applied to 142 checks totaling $2,840. 82 of these transactions ($1,640) occurred between 3:30 PM and 4:30 PM or post-midnight closing, applied to single-item beverage and sandwich orders rather than full guest table checks.
Intersection
Time-of-day clustering, item composition (single meal items), and check ownership demonstrate that managerial override codes are being utilized during shift turnover and closing rather than during dining service.
Inferred
Shift supervisors and lead servers are using the general manager comp code to record shift meals and employee break allowances because no designated employee meal code exists in the active POS configuration. This represents procedural shortcutting and training deficiency rather than intentional theft.
Confidence
Moderate. Strong transactional pattern (time, item type, check size), but requires human floor confirmation that employees received these items as valid shift meals.

Candidate Interventions

Signal separates who touched the transaction from who caused the problem: Configure dedicated 'Shift Meal' discount key with preset operational limits, update manager SOPs, and reserve MGR-COMP-99 strictly for documented guest recovery.

05 // What to Test First

The Zero-Disruption 48-Hour Staging Protocol

Rather than disrupting kitchen workflow or calling emergency manager meetings, operators should execute tests in order of least friction:

  1. Day 1: Update the uncapped 10% POS rule to cap discount at $5 on checks above $50 (Finding F-02). Zero staff training required.
  2. Day 1: Adjust prices on the 4 high-frequency modifiers (+Bacon to $2.00, +Truffle Fries to $2.75, +Cheese to $1.00, +GF Bun to $2.00) in POS backend (Finding F-03).
  3. Day 3: Configure dedicated 'Staff Meal' discount key; communicate policy shift to lead supervisors at line huddle (Finding F-04).
  4. Friday Evening: GM conducts 60-minute pass audit between 6:30 PM and 7:30 PM to verify pass ticket latency before adjusting prep call times (Finding F-01).
