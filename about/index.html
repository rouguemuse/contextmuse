01 // Experience

Operating Background

I learned systems where failure has a cost.   Restaurants. Production lines. Customer operations. Incomplete information.   The work was never just collecting data. It was figuring out what mattered.

Years managing high-volume kitchen and pastry production lines built an instinct for resource prioritization, quality control, multi-workstream coordination, and adoption management under pressure.

In a professional kitchen, an unoptimized workflow leads immediately to wasted prep, delayed service, and lost margins. That experience shapes every system I design: it must be simple, reliable, and built around how humans actually behave — not the ideal scenario in a planning document.

The transition into operational intelligence and AI tooling was not a career pivot. It was the same problem with a wider set of tools.

02 // Focus

What I Do

01
Untangle complexity

Taxonomy design, source mapping, chronology recovery, metadata contradictions, and record structure — making the disorganized navigable.

02
Design decision systems

Prioritization logic, weighted scoring models, manager review queues, and human-in-the-loop workflows that support better judgment — not replace it.

03
Turn operations into tools

Interactive dashboards, intake interfaces, user flow mapping, and technical implementation planning — connecting what people know to what a system can do.

03 // Philosophy

How I Think

I start with the actual mess.

The handoffs, conflicting records, repeated work, missing context, and decisions people keep postponing. That is where the real problem lives.

Systems should accelerate judgment, not impersonate it.

The goal is to help someone understand what matters, why it matters, and what to do next — not to automate away the person who has to make the call.

I build for real operating conditions.

Incomplete records, changing priorities, tired people, limited time, and systems that still have to work when no one follows the ideal process.
