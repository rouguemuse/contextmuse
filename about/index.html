01 // Focus

What I Do

I am strongest at the point where real-world operations meet technology. I identify what people are actually trying to accomplish, locate the information and workflow failures preventing it, and turn those findings into structured tools, decision systems, documentation, and implementation plans.

My work begins where “we have the data” stops being the same thing as “we know what to do.” I build for the moment when five people have five versions of what is happening and someone still has to decide what to do next.

02 // Philosophy

How I Think

I start with the actual mess.

I look at the handoffs, conflicting information, repeated work, missing context, and decisions people keep postponing.

I do not build dashboards just to display more information.

The point is to help someone understand what matters, why it matters, and what to do next.

I build for real operating conditions.

Incomplete records, changing priorities, tired people, limited time, and systems that still have to work when no one follows the ideal process.

03 // Application

Where I’m Most Useful

Specific transition points where operational leadership meets technical execution.

Untangling complex information

Taxonomy design, source mapping, chronology recovery, metadata contradictions, and record structure.

Designing decision systems

Prioritization logic, weighted scoring models, manager review queues, and human-in-the-loop workflows.

Turning operations into products

Interactive dashboards, intake interfaces, user flow mapping, and technical implementation planning.

04 // Experience

Operating Background

“I learned systems in kitchens and restaurants—where the answer has to work at 7:30 on a Friday, not just look good in a presentation.”

Years of managing high-volume kitchen and pastry production lines taught me resource prioritization, strict quality control, multi-workstream coordination, and adoption management.

In a professional kitchen, an unoptimized workflow leads immediately to wasted prep, delayed times, and lost margins. That experience drives how I design digital tools: they must be simple, reliable, and built around actual human behavior rather than idealistic scenarios.
